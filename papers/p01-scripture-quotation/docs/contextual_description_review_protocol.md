# Contextual Description Review Protocol

Pre-committed protocol for the independent scholarly validation of the twenty
contextual descriptions used in FID-056 Paper 01. Written and released before
any reviewer response is collected.

## Why this review exists

The study's contextual-description prompts test whether a system can identify
which passage is meant before quoting it. That test is only meaningful if a
competent human reader, given the same description and no other information,
lands on the passage we expected. Without that check, a low
reference-identification rate could mean the systems failed, or it could mean
our descriptions were bad.

The validation reported in the working manuscript does not establish this. Its
limitations were, in the manuscript's own words, that the reviewer was an AI
system and the same reviewing identity that authored the initial critique, and
that only six of the twenty descriptions received blinded treatment. This
protocol replaces that check.

## Reviewer

One or more reviewers with graduate-level training in biblical studies,
theology, or a closely related field, independent of the drafting of the target
pack and of the study team's analysis decisions. Reviewers are compensated at a
standard consulting rate. Compensation is not contingent on the level of
agreement observed.

## Materials

Generated by `scripts/build_contextual_review_packet.py`:

| Artifact | Contents |
|---|---|
| `review/blinded_contextual_descriptions.md` | All twenty descriptions, deterministically shuffled, no references |
| `review/reviewer_response_template.csv` | Response form |
| `review/packet_key.json` | Position-to-reference mapping; withheld from reviewers until responses are returned |

## Blinding

The reviewer receives descriptions only. Withheld: the expected references, the
passage strata, all model outputs and study results, and — critically — which
six descriptions were revised during instrument development. The six revised
items cannot be identified within the packet, so they cannot receive different
scrutiny from the fourteen originals.

Item order is shuffled under a fixed seed so that the packet is reproducible
but does not follow canonical biblical order, which would otherwise let a
reviewer infer answers positionally.

This is reviewer-blind, not double-blind: the study team knows the key when
scoring. Scoring is mechanical (`scripts/score_contextual_review.py`) and the
rules below are fixed in advance, which is what that mechanization is for.

## What the reviewer is asked

1. **Reference** — the passage intended, including the verse range.
2. **Ambiguity** — whether more than one passage is defensibly identifiable,
   and which alternatives.
3. **Confidence** — high / medium / low.
4. **Comment** — anything misleading, anachronistic, theologically loaded, or
   dependent on a particular translation tradition.

Reviewers are asked not to consult study materials and not to answer by
querying an AI assistant.

## Scoring rules, fixed in advance

Three quantities are reported separately and are not combined into a single
score:

- **Exact agreement** — reviewer's reference matches the expected reference.
- **Span disagreement** — same book and chapter, different verse boundaries.
- **Identification disagreement** — a different passage.

Ambiguity flags are reported separately and are never counted as failures. A
description flagged ambiguous is a finding about the instrument whether or not
the reviewer also arrived at the expected reference.

## Commitments

1. Every disagreement and every ambiguity flag is reported verbatim, whatever
   the agreement rate turns out to be.
2. Descriptions will not be revised and re-reviewed in order to raise the
   agreement figure. If descriptions prove ambiguous, that is published as a
   limitation on the contextual-prompt results.
3. Primary study scores are already frozen. This review cannot change any
   reported quantitative result; it can only change how the contextual-prompt
   findings are interpreted and qualified.
4. If a description is found ambiguous, the affected target's contextual-prompt
   results are reported with that qualification attached rather than dropped.

## Reporting

Results replace the `Contextual-description review` limitation paragraph in the
manuscript and are stated as: number of reviewers, exact agreement, span
disagreements, identification disagreements, ambiguity flags, and the full
disagreement list. The completed response file and this protocol are released
in the public package.
