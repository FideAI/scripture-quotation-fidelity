# Claims Boundary

Paper 02 estimates how two instructions affect observed use of a designated
Scripture source tool: a system-level requirement to consult the source and a
user-level request to avoid tools and answer from memory.

Results apply only to the named endpoints, upstream routes, run dates, 20
targets, two prompt families, Berean Standard Bible fixtures, five
repetitions, tool contract, and scoring protocol. They do not establish:

- a general ranking of model vendors or families;
- behavior after endpoint, routing, or policy changes;
- theological correctness, contextual interpretation, or pastoral safety;
- that invoking a tool guarantees a correct reference or exact final quote;
- effects for other tools, source domains, languages, or restricted editions;
- that a system's private reasoning or intent can be inferred from its trace;
- training-data membership or the cause of parametric recall; or
- deployment readiness, legal compliance, or endorsement of a product.

The primary endpoint records an externally observable source call. It does not
claim to measure trust, confidence, humility, or any other internal state.

Target-cluster intervals condition on the six purposively selected routes. They
represent target-panel uncertainty for those evaluated endpoints, not
uncertainty over a population of models or providers. Route-as-unit and
leave-one-route-out analyses are generalization sensitivities.

The contextual descriptions received an AI-assisted review and a blinded
self-reapproval of six revised items. A separately specified credentialed,
external biblical-scholar review remains open. The paper does not claim that
the contextual prompts have received independent human scholarly validation.
